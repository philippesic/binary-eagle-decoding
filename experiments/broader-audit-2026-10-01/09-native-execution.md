# Native low-bit inference execution audit

2026-10-01. Source: parent `f0bb92dda687d908b3cffe084510b3be757df54f`, native `b4e366d4f0a30cac07f14d51c54c5b1329b3f485`. This is source/research review only. No builds, tests, models, accelerators, remote actions or sealed-final inputs were used. Q4_0 is the primary acceptance/latency/throughput target; target and verifier stay fixed. RTX 2080 Ti remains paused. Training prefix batching and A1 autograd are outside this report.

## Verdict and completed work

Prepare an explicit fitted-sign/scale **Q1_0 export bridge first**, then a **joint packed gate/up projection** prototype. These are narrow routes into the existing runtime. A persistent decoder or general CUDA replacement is unjustified before measurements show remaining kernel gaps or repeated weight traffic matter.

The [Phase 1B completion audit](../w1-phase1b-completion-audit.md) closes shared activation packing, compact vocabulary sampling, bounded resident state, K/V-only catch-up, integer reduction cleanup and diagnostic event accounting. Shared packing already produces one activation operand for Q/K/V and gate/up. It does not combine their dot launches. The [runtime report](../eagle-runtime-optimizations.md#final-order-balanced-selector-results) measures row-A4 shared packing at 1.00312× decode, warp reduction at 1.00289×, and resident row-A16 at 0.99132× on SM120. All 180 pairs preserved behavior and all 120 blocks launched CUDA graphs. Those small packing/reduction observations do not establish general gains. Compact sampling and K/V-only catch-up have separate bounded positive results; they cannot be added into an unmeasured combined speedup. Binary MMA also already exists. Early per-round caps were previously reviewed, not completed: current EAGLE stops its generation loop at global `params.n_max` (`common/speculative.cpp:1020`), while the common wrapper truncates to `dp.n_max` afterward (`3117–3120`). No early-cap selector is present. Unused-head and K/V-only pruning do not implement draft stopping.

Native Q1_0 storage and CPU/CUDA kernels exist. A naive all-nine Q1_0 GGUF and CPU smoke were completed in the [Prism audit](../prism-quantization-research-2026-09-25.md). The new work is exporting **learned/fitted** signs and group scales directly into that format with explicit activation semantics. Current `scripts/export_recurrent_binary.py` exports custom I32 signs/F32 scales, while `w1a1.cu:350–388` allows grouped scales only at A16 and dispatches ordered scalar sign-add. Thus a fitted Q1_0 deployment bridge is not implemented; neither is grouped A1 partial-dot execution.

## Arithmetic and traffic bounds

The archived [SM75 trace](../rtx2080ti-quantization-suite.md#packing-inclusive-cuda-component-profiles) identifies these real matrices: fusion 2560×7680; Q/K/V 4096/1024/1024×5120; O 2560×4096; gate/up 9728×2560; down 2560×9728; head 32000×2560. Across all nine there are 218,234,880 weights. The following are calculated matrix payloads, excluding embedding, maps, norms, caches and allocator padding:

| Format | Bytes for nine matrices | Relative weight payload |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| F16 | 436,469,760 | 16 bits/weight |
| Q4_0, 18 bytes/32 | 122,757,120 | 4.5 bits/weight |
| Q1_0, 18 bytes/128 | 30,689,280 | 1.125 bits/weight |
| Custom row W1/F32 scale | 27,540,480 | signs plus 65,280 scales |
| Custom group128 W1/F32 scale | 34,099,200 | 1.25 bits/weight |

One full nine-linear sweep has 218.235 million logical MACs per column, or 6,819,840 binary word dots at A1; N=5 multiplies arithmetic by five. This is a shape budget, not a speculative-round budget: fusion runs separately, head pruning changes selected rows, and recurrent proposal steps cannot be freely stacked. For uncached weight reads the payload floor is `bytes/bandwidth`; reducing Q4_0 to Q1_0 offers a fourfold **weight-byte** reduction, not fourfold end-to-end speed.

The custom A1 kernel assigns four rows per CTA and a separate grid-Y column (`w1a1.cu:49–90`). Its source repeats weight loads for N columns and reads packed activations per output row. Cache can absorb much of this traffic. A tile that retains one weight word while processing N=2–5 columns can reduce logical weight loads by at most N, with more registers/reductions. At N=1 it has no reuse advantage. Joining Q/K/V reads three different matrices: it saves launches and common activation work, **not** their 3.93 MB of sign weights. Gate/up similarly retain 6.23 MB of distinct signs. Their common F32 inputs are only 20,480 and 10,240 bytes per column.

The old SM75 software trace measures N=1 A1 pack/dot medians of 5.568/3.632 µs for M=1024, 5.632/7.856 for M=4096, and 4.657/10.096 for M=9728. Head is 4.672/26.465 µs; N=37 head dot rises to 884.952 µs. These instrumented historical kernels show a small-layer packing/launch hypothesis and a large-N reuse hypothesis. They do not measure current N=2–5 launch gaps. Actual binary MMA tied portable head decode at 1.002× on SM75; INT8/INT4 MMA lost despite winning larger-N component cases. Peak instruction throughput is therefore a poor selection rule here.

## Ranked two-day preparation tasks

**1. Fitted Q1_0 codec and contract oracle.** Implement a direct block writer for the trained sign bits and rounded FP16 group128 scales, preserving Q/K row permutation, draft vocabulary mapping and untouched tensors. Do not quantize a reconstructed dense matrix through the generic meanabs packer: it would refit scales and can erase negative signs in zero-scale groups. Build tiny CPU codec/oracle fixtures for zero scales, signed zero, finite scale range and all real K widths, which are divisible by 128. Later CPU compilation should verify ordinary dense-name Q1 loading and graph construction, without full weights. This audit executes none of that preparation.

`ggml-common.h:180–185` stores 128 bits plus one F16 scale. `vecdotq.cuh:678–724` expands signs to signed bytes, uses eight DP4A operations per 32-element chunk, then multiplies the Q1 and Q8_1 chunk scales. `mmvq.cu` and `mmq.cu` already dispatch Q1. Q8_1 introduces block32 activation quantization and floating accumulation between chunks; it is neither custom whole-token A8 nor ordered A16. Export scale rounding and activation conversion must be isolated in separate oracle comparisons. The eventual CUDA gate is same-sign/group-scale custom A16 versus Q1 with those differences explicitly measured, followed by native acceptance and throughput against Q4_0. Stop if Q8_1 changes decisions materially or the zero-draft-time upper bound cannot beat Q4_0. This is the highest value preparation because it removes a known slow grouped scalar implementation without inventing a kernel stack.

**2. Joint gate/up packed operation, then Q/K/V.** Prepare one CPU graph operation with two weight/scale inputs and the existing shared activation pack. First preserve separate outputs and exact integer reductions/scaling order; then optionally fuse `silu(gate)*up` after the two scaled dots. Q/K/V is harder because output sizes and RoPE/cache consumers differ. Preserve stage-capture fallback and K/V-only eligibility rather than forcing the full joint Q projection during catch-up.

This can remove one gate/up dot launch, or two Q/K/V dot launches. Fusing the FFN epilogue can avoid two 38,912-byte F32 outputs and their reads per column, subject to existing pointwise fusion. The runtime already fuses ordinary MUL_MAT/GLU patterns and SILU/MUL; custom W1 operations do not match the ordinary matmul patterns. Consequently this is incremental packed support, with a Q4_0 control that already has substantial fusion. Later CUDA A/B must preserve raw integer dots, state/cache and proposal decisions, and measure packing-inclusive N=1–5 latency plus request/decode rates. Stop after two kernel layouts if the paired draft gain is below 3% or disappears against the graph-enabled control; do not promote from a kernel-only win.

**3. N=2–5 reuse and graph-stability decision packet.** Prepare a tiny scalar multi-column oracle and inspect graph fingerprints/counters by process/draft shape. The backend already keys graphs by first-node identity, compares tensor properties and requires two stable calls; changed properties reset warmup (`ggml-cuda.cu:2888–2940,4737–4766`). Existing UID reuse avoids repeated property checks. Inventory presence proves graphs execute, not that every recurrent shape replays efficiently. Recommend a shape-specific cache only if later nonintrusive traces show repeated warmup resets/rebuilds dominate. Never suppress pointer/stride/cache-position invalidation. A bounded tiled A1 kernel is conditional on repeated N>1 weight loads, not a persistent full decoder. Host sampling and target verification break cross-step persistence; preserving their semantics outweighs saving a speculative graph launch.

## Primary research and transfer limits

[MARLIN paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11743v1) and [author code](https://github.com/IST-DASLab/marlin) demonstrate weight reuse, pipelining and striped scheduling for W4A16 up to medium batches. The code requires SM80+ and its published A10/Ampere/Ada evidence does not establish SM75 or SM120 performance. Borrow the reuse principle, not its kernel or advertised speedup. [FLUTE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10960) reports 2–4× kernel gains for LUT-quantized small batches; [author code](https://github.com/HanGuo97/flute) specializes by hardware, shape, bits and group size. Its wider-activation LUT arithmetic and tuning cost weaken direct transfer to binary POPC. [BitNet's GPU author code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/BitNet/main/gpu/README.md) uses permuted W2A8/DP4A GEMV and reports A100 measurements; that is useful decode-layout precedent, not an SM75/SM120 W1A1 result. [NVIDIA's llama.cpp graph study](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/optimizing-llama-cpp-ai-inference-with-cuda-graphs/) shows inter-kernel gaps can shrink through replay, which this fork already implements. None establishes a benefit from another persistent scheduler here.

All later performance gates require separate same-device anchors, alternating order, five repetitions, measured emissions/round and unchanged target/verifier. Apply `docs/EVALUATION.md`'s zero-draft-time screening bound before substantial CUDA investment. Better storage or faster dots cannot repair the observed acceptance deficit by themselves.
