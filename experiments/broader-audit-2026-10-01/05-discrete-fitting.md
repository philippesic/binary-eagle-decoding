## Verdict and project evidence

**Pursue one bounded fusion sign-and-scale reconstruction proposal; defer wholesale imports of published binary optimizers.** Direct discrete fitting can find a better deployment-compatible initialization without STE or recurrent backpropagation. It is a complement to the running QAT control, not evidence that QAT is unnecessary.

The completed [scale screen](../binary-scale-fitting-5080.md) optimized scales but retained original signs across 218,234,880 weights. Group-fitted D reached 0.425 accepted drafts/round versus Q4_0's 1.042, with especially weak deeper-prefix survival. Fusion is unusually promising: its calibration relative squared output error fell from 0.974 with row-mean scales to 0.159 with fitted group scales. Nevertheless, none of fusion's 2,560 group solves met the fixed convergence tolerance. A stronger scale-only control is therefore essential before attributing gains to sign changes. The previous [Prism audit](../prism-quantization-research-2026-09-25.md) already derived the correct single-flip algebra; released Bonsai signs differing from naive packing do not identify a secret optimizer.

## Objective, exact flips and useful statistics

For one output row, let calibration columns be `x_j`, binary coefficients `q_j = a_g(j) s_j`, signs `s_j ∈ {−1,+1}`, nonnegative scales, teacher output `y`, residual `r = Xq−y`, and `L = ||r||²/N`. Inputs are uncentered. For a fixed-scale flip of bit j, `δ_j = −2a_g(j)s_j` and

`ΔL_j = −4a_g(j)s_j (x_jᵀr/N) + 4a_g(j)² (x_jᵀx_j/N)`.

This follows by expanding `||r+δ_j x_j||²`; it is exact for real-arithmetic linear reconstruction. Accept only a negative change beyond a declared numerical margin, then update `r ← r+δ_j x_j`. With `H=XᵀX/N` and `c=Xᵀr/N`, update correlations as `c ← c+δ_j H[:,j]`. A batch F has

`ΔL_F = Σ_j ΔL_j + 2Σ_{j<k∈F} δ_jδ_k H_jk`.

Thus individually improving stale proposals can jointly worsen loss. Sequential updates, exact small-block enumeration, or a joint residual check resolve this; independent output rows can run in parallel. Two-bit candidates can escape some single-flip minima. Under diagonal H and `y=Xw`, source-weight signs admit no improving fixed-scale single flip: covariance is the specific opportunity, not a generic claim that binary signs are poorly chosen.

Alternate accepted sign moves with a nonnegative scale solve. For a row scale, `z=Xs` gives `a=max(0,zᵀy/zᵀz)`; ridge toward `a₀` replaces numerator/denominator by `zᵀy/N+λa₀` and `zᵀz/N+λ`. Group scales use the existing constrained `ZᵀZ`, `Zᵀy` solver and direct exported-point fallback. Keep one fixed ridge definition during each alternating experiment; changing its normalization as signs change changes the objective. Zero-scale groups cannot move through fixed-scale flips, so explicitly report them and allow the next scale solve or a small jointly evaluated sign/scale proposal.

A full 218M-parameter Hessian is unnecessary and impossible here: dense F32 storage would approach 190 petabytes. Linear reconstruction has independent output rows and a shared K×K input Gram per layer. Fusion's K=7,680 Gram occupies about 236 MB; the largest K=9,728 Gram about 379 MB. Memory is manageable, but constructing fusion's Gram costs roughly 181 billion MACs and dense factorizations add cubic work. With only 3,072 calibration examples these Grams are rank deficient, requiring damping for inverse-based methods.

Retaining fusion's 3,072×7,680 X costs about 94 MB and permits exact residual checks without H. Streaming accumulates H and `Xᵀy`; alternatively diagonal-plus-rank-128 sketches store approximately 4 MB of fusion covariance factors, but produce approximate rankings. They require exact checks against X before accepting moves. Existing fixed-sign group statistics are insufficient after a flip: Z changes. A sketch cannot resurrect correlations or teacher targets discarded from an old scale report.

For A16 weight-only fitting, X is the actual boundary-cast input and `y=Xw` isolates weight error. For A1, use `U_tj=β_t sign(X_tj)` as the design matrix, retain the ordinary teacher output y, and replace X/H by U/`UᵀU/N` throughout. This optimizes combined activation-and-weight error. A16 covariance/sign improvements need not transfer: lost activation magnitudes alter both correlations and the attainable solution. Original-sign local optimality under diagonal H no longer follows when teacher and student use different inputs.

## What primary research actually supports

[GPTQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.17323) uses second-order error compensation, primarily demonstrating 3–4-bit weight-only quality. Its [author code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IST-DASLab/gptq/main/gptq.py) damps H, factors its inverse and compensates unfixed coordinates after quantizing each coordinate. Its OPT-175B three-bit single-A100 latency result compares against FP16 on five A100s; it is neither a binary EAGLE nor SM75 result. For our fixed `±a` codebook, compensation can change future signs without changing deployment format. However, shared scales and large irreversible one-bit jumps weaken the ordinary GPTQ analogy. Treat compensated quantization as an initializer, then evaluate/refine its actual binary outputs; do not claim its continuous compensation guarantees survive final binarization.

[BiLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.04291v2), with [author code](https://github.com/Aaronhuang-778/BiLLM), reports LLaMA2-70B WikiText2 perplexity 8.41 at 1.08 average weight bits and roughly 30-minute 7B binarization on one A100-80GB. It combines salient-column residual binarization, magnitude-dependent grouping and block error compensation. Residuals require multiple binary terms for selected weights; group identification also has storage/execution implications. This is near-one-bit weight-only compression, not the current single-sign W1A1 arithmetic, and task/perplexity retention does not establish draft acceptance.

[ARB-LLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03129v2) alternates means/scales/signs; ARB-X incorporates calibration, ARB-RC adds column scales, and final configurations add bitmaps and salient residual terms. Experiments use an A800-80GB, 15 iterations and 128 C4 samples; reported LLaMA3-70B WikiText2 perplexity is 11.10 at 1.06 average bits. The [author launch commands](https://github.com/ZHITENGLI/ARB-LLM) explicitly enable second-order grouping. Learned row offsets contribute `μΣx_j`, which is input dependent, not an ordinary bias; column scales and residual terms likewise require runtime support. The useful transferable idea is alternating optimization, not importing its headline result into our existing format.

[PV-Tuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.14852v2) and its [author implementation](https://github.com/Vahe1994/AQLM/tree/pv-tuning) alternate continuous parameters with discrete assignments, using subspaces to permit meaningful code changes. Its approximately one-bit results use vector codebooks rather than one binary sign per weight; Llama2-7B WikiText2 perplexity is 8.28 at 1.02 bits. Published 70B training needs eight A100s or offloading; the longest run took two days to surpass baselines and eight days to converge. Full PV-Tuning is therefore not a two-day CPU PTQ substitute. Small candidate subspaces with actual objective checks are useful inspiration.

The counterargument is strong: local reconstruction can optimize irrelevant directions and ordinary-drafter imitation can preserve the wrong token rankings. If useful flips are absent or held-out recurrent behavior worsens, retain fitted scales and the running STE baseline; targeted output-loss training is the better alternative. First gate exported arithmetic on bounded same-input native replays, including A16 casts or A1 raw-input meanabs/sign packing and ordered dot→row-scale→token-scale multiplication. Advance only on native acceptance and matched end-to-end timing against Q4_0, with the target/verifier fixed; CPU reconstruction cannot validate 2080 Ti performance.

Binary sign fitting is worth one small, controlled experiment because scale fitting never tested different signs. Fusion provides the clearest opportunity and the smallest practical first target. Published near-one-bit methods often buy quality with extra residuals, offsets or codebooks that our current binary path does not execute. A lower reconstruction error can improve an initialization without improving accepted draft prefixes. The decisive result remains native acceptance and total throughput against Q4_0.
