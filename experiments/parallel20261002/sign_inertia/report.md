# Binary sign movement and inertia audit

The synthetic gates distinguish an inadequate movement budget, recurrent
chatter, and hard binary infeasibility. Lower fresh latent magnitude permits
earlier useful decisions on one reachable task, but does not yield a uniformly
stable recurrent recipe. No live optimizer change or recipe selection follows.

## Scope and reproducibility

CPU, macOS arm64, Torch 2.14.0, float32; source base
`6c613039d2c6bef3f6f7b5c6e9bf5f8cbccac2e8`. The
[manifest](manifest.json) pins seven imported/core source hashes and the raw
result hash. The [runner](../../../research/parallel20261002/sign_inertia/reference/audit.py)
uses actual `RowBinaryLinear(W1AxContract(1))`, initialization,
`make_binary_optimizer`, gradient transforms, projection, and packed
`SignFlipDiagnostics`, sampled every update. It does not instantiate a trainer,
read project data, or exercise a GPU/native verifier.

The declared main budget is 64 updates, constant sign LR 0.001, scale LR
0.00001, AdamW (0.9,0.999), epsilon 1e-8, no decay, clipping limits 1.0.
There are 96 controlled trajectories: two tasks, scales 0.01/1/100, initial
latent magnitudes 0.5/0.02, frozen/joint scales, and baseline/weight_unit/
per_family/combined. Three fixed controls add infeasible XOR and SGD with
momentum 0/0.9. This compares existing options; the API has no latent-centering
control. Frozen scale means clearing its gradient, preserving optimizer
ownership and parameter identity.

The main grid omits production warmup to isolate crossing and gradient rules.
`ContinuousConfig` defaults to 100-step linear warmup and samples persistent
diagnostics every 100 steps. A separate actual-optimizer constant-gradient
control applies that exact LR schedule: 64 updates displace 0.0208, so magnitude
0.5 stays positive (0.479199976), while magnitude 0.02 crosses at update 63.
The production 2,000-pair zero-flip warning is an observation threshold; this
audit does not infer that a real EAGLE budget is inadequate.

## Exact capacity and decision gates

The linear fixture has four inputs `(+++),(+--),(-+-),(--+)`. Exhaustive eight
sign-row enumeration gives exactly one strict solution, `(+,-,+)`, at every
positive scale tested. The no-bias XOR labels `+--+` have no strict solution
among four sign rows, for any positive scale: opposite inputs force opposite
outputs while their labels agree. Ties count as failures.

The recurrent fixture has four three-step input sequences, initial state 0.25,
one binary row over `(input, previous state)`, frozen bias +0.15, and attached
`tanh` state. Labels are generated by hard signs `(+,-)` at scale 1. Enumeration
of all four sign states finds one complete solution at scales 1 and 100,
and none at scale 0.01 because the frozen bias dominates. The scalar oracle uses
the production A1 **mean absolute** activation scale. Independent validation
caught and corrected an earlier max-absolute assumption and a vacuous
all-positive label fixture before final results. All corrected hard logits and
decisions are checked against the actual API.

Logits are the binary class pair `(z,-z)`. Reported margin is `y*z` (half the
class-logit gap); CE is `softplus(-2*y*z)`. A useful flip must increase strict
correct decisions or improve minimum margin without losing decisions; a wrong
flip loses decisions or worsens minimum margin without gaining decisions.
Both compare old/new signs at the **same post-update scale**, with 1e-5 margin
tolerance, preventing learned scale movement from receiving sign credit.
Hard prefix is a synthetic consecutive-decision count, not native acceptance.

At scale 1, the four Adam controls give identical frozen-scale linear and
recurrent trajectories. The default 0.5 inertia stays 0.436 from zero after
64 updates despite nonzero gradients and moments. The lower-inertia linear
case produces one sustained useful sign, CE 1.09581→0.095814 and synthetic
prefix 1→4. Across linear scales 0.01/1/100, baseline reaches full decisions
at 21/20/20 steps; margins remain scale-dependent 0.01/1/100. SGD reaches the
scale-1 linear solution at 22; SGD momentum 0.9 reaches it at 7. Those are
crossing observations, not evidence to prefer momentum on EAGLE.

The lower-inertia recurrent case's first success is unstable: three wrong
flip-backs and only six fully correct final-tail updates. Its final sustained
disagreement counter is 1, showing that this counter describes adjacent
observations, not long-term success. Joint learned scale with `weight_unit`
delays first useful flip to 21, ends 8/12 correct, and gives only four fully
correct tail updates; baseline/per_family end 12/12 with six. Thus scale-aware
gradients do not dominate baseline. At scale 100, the recurrent surrogate
produces raw sign gradients `[+1104.036,-1104.036]` before clipping and one
harmful sign crossing; CE worsens 0.37718→80.34002. Exact enumeration still
contains a perfect binary solution, separating this optimization failure from
capacity.

Joint scale learning can reduce CE with zero sign or decision improvement:
high-inertia linear scale 1 reduces CE 1.09581→1.09529; low-scale recurrent
reduces CE 0.64934→0.64902 while keeping 8/12 decisions. Its small scale change
does not close the fixed-bias reachability gap in this budget. At zero scale,
`weight_unit` cannot revive the sign gradient; scale revival remains separate.

## Gradient and optimizer-state evidence

The raw ignored output records each master's gradient before/after transform,
clipped scale norm, master update, scale, minimum latent distance, all hard
logits/margins, moments, exact flip counters, and old-sign counterfactual.
For frozen linear scale 1, update 1 sign gradient is
`[-0.0608378, +0.9391623, -0.0608378]`; Adam moments are
`m=[-0.00608378,+0.0939162,-0.00608378]`,
`v=[3.70124e-6,8.82026e-4,3.70124e-6]`; master movement is approximately
`[+0.001,-0.001,+0.001]`. These are identical for both initial magnitudes,
while only the smaller magnitude can cross within 64 updates.

With a constant positive gradient `g` and no projection hit, ideal Adam's
displacement per update is `lr*g/(abs(g)+eps)`; fixed gradient scaling mostly
cancels except epsilon. SGD moves `lr*g`. Momentum SGD at update `t` moves
`lr*g*(1-mu**t)/(1-mu)` from zero initial buffer. Exact zero remains positive;
first negative crossing requires cumulative displacement strictly greater than
the initial magnitude. Independent tests verify those assumptions with exactly
representable SGD crossing examples, Adam's float32 residual, and momentum
buffers. Changing recurrent gradients, clipping multipliers, scale, or hitting
projection invalidates the constant-gradient prediction.

## Recommendation and acceptance

Add telemetry before considering a recipe change: per-family clipped norms,
sign master displacement and near-zero distance, LR integral including warmup,
flip/flip-back histories paired with hard decision margins/prefixes, and a
short persistence window. Keep the diagnostic observation gap in every report;
sampling every other step can miss four alternating flips entirely. Current
trainer already records near-zero/projection fractions and sampled flips;
the missing distinction is useful persistent hard decisions versus movement.

No candidate recipe is selected: lower inertia passes one useful-movement gate
but fails recurrent persistence and nuisance-scale robustness. A future owner
test should falsify any candidate with native current-student decisions and
Q4_0-relative acceptance/throughput under a fixed budget. No real data,
held-out/final set, training recipe, verifier precision, or preparation process
was accessed or changed. CPU evidence does not establish SM75/native speed.
