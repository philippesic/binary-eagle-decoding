# QAT execution advisory: recover useful proposals before broadening training

Local-only analysis, 2026-09-25. No web search, model execution, training, GPU access, repository edits, or active-goal changes. References below are repository-relative `path:line` locations. Recommendations are proposed experiments, not implemented changes. The current active goal remains the W1Ax activation-precision study (`docs/STATUS.md:3`; `docs/goals/w1ax-activation-precision-suite.md:8`).

## Recommendation

Execute the already proposed target-aligned head pilot after the W1Ax study and alignment audit, with online development acceptance selecting the checkpoint. Treat it as a diagnosis of training supervision and deployment fidelity, not a likely sufficient route to beat Q4_0. Preserve the existing quantizer in the first run. Add a narrowly bounded reconstruction control if the user approves the extra arm; then permit at most one state-refresh continuation. Do not respond to another KL/acceptance disagreement by increasing steps or enabling all nine binary layers.

The strongest case for this priority is that the original pilot never supervised target-verifier choices: its teacher was the old draft head evaluated on cached draft features. Its loss and selection criterion were both cached KL. The native head-only path is also slower per draft call than the Q4_0 anchor, so acceptance recovery must ultimately be judged against two different throughput denominators.

## What is established, and what remains a hypothesis

1. **Established failure of one recipe, not of QAT.** The RTX 5080 BF16 pilot improved cached KL from 2.50634 to 1.01696 and old-draft top-1 agreement from 0.47667 to 0.54167. Its one held-out W1A1 evaluation decreased accepted drafts/round from 1.67668 to 1.56522 (`experiments/qat-head-pilot-results.md:32–48,80–85`). The report records 500 steps at batch 64 over 32,768 cached training rows: about 32,000 row presentations, less than one full pass. This is not evidence that enough optimization or data diversity has been exhausted; equally, the improvement in the wrong validation proxy is not evidence that a longer run would help acceptance.
2. **The teacher mismatch is source-confirmed.** Both training and validation construct teacher logits as `F.linear(batch, teacher)` where `teacher` is the original BF16 draft-head weight (`scripts/train_qat_head.py:340–359,453–455,495–499`; capture source at `scripts/capture_qat_head_inputs.py:310–320`). Neither target next-token labels nor verifier logits enter this loss. KL is computed over all 32,000 draft outputs, not the target's complete vocabulary (`scripts/train_qat_head.py:319–337`). Checkpoints are selected only by this KL (`scripts/train_qat_head.py:517–530`). This is a mismatch between the training proxy and the desired metric; its causal share in the acceptance decrease is unmeasured.
3. **The cached distribution lacks the information needed for target alignment.** The hook saves prompt/trajectory/head-call ordinals and row number, with head-call ordinal labeled tree depth (`scripts/capture_qat_head_inputs.py:189–212`). It does not save parent node/path, selected ancestry, absolute positions, verifier row, target token, or reachability. The existing alignment audit explicitly identifies the missing association and the seed-token shift (`experiments/qat-revisit-plan.md:49–73`). A correct label cannot be reconstructed from ordinal alone.
4. **Row-uniform sampling is not accepted-path sampling.** The reservoir samples rows uniformly within each prompt (`scripts/capture_qat_head_inputs.py:87–115`) and a global cap subsequently samples records (`:215–227`). A tree with more rows at deeper or off-path nodes gives those nodes more loss weight than a root or accepted-prefix state. Actual depth counts and the harm, if any, must be measured. Also, the two trajectories are assigned to alternating prompts, rather than both trajectories being captured for each prompt (`:82–84,343–360`). Consequently trajectory and prompt are confounded in the first capture, even though category counts are balanced. New paired captures on the same training prompts would support a cleaner distribution-shift diagnosis.
5. **The latent full-precision head is not a fair quality anchor.** Its held-out acceptance declined to 1.81331 (`experiments/qat-head-pilot-results.md:84`). That does show export changed useful behavior, but the latent matrix is optimized through a binary forward and has no requirement to remain a good dense head. This decline alone neither proves a bug nor justifies adding a dense-head loss. Keep the original untouched FP16 head as the anchor; use dense execution of QAT latents only as a diagnostic.
6. **Native success remains harder than head recovery.** On the frozen RTX 2080 Ti suite, ordinary FP16/Q4_0/head-W1A1 decoded at 82.488/91.508/74.813 tokens/s, with 1.168/1.182/0.860 accepted drafts/round (`experiments/rtx2080ti-quantization-suite.md:185–195`). Their logged draft-call costs were 6.471/4.178/5.263 ms per verification round (`:199–210`). These are host draft spans under differing trajectories, not isolated identical-input costs; isolated target verification cost is missing. They warn that recovering merely FP16-like acceptance may still lose to Q4_0. They do not establish a numerical acceptance break-even threshold.

## Training objective: match the proposal decision and keep the verifier fixed

For the current greedy task, use the unchanged verifier's next-token choice at the exact draft parent prefix as the hard target. The prefix includes the target-selected seed before the first head prediction and the appropriate ancestor tokens for later predictions. Save both the actual verifier label and, where checked, a canonical full-prefix target label; disagreements are numerical/state diagnostics, not permission to choose whichever teacher gives better acceptance. Never alter verifier tolerance or target sampling to make QAT look better.

A bounded first objective is

`L = weighted_mean[ CE(q_theta(state), mapped_target_id) ] + lambda * KL(q_original_draft || q_theta)`.

Prespecify one small lambda, for example 0.05, as an experimental choice, not an empirically established optimum. Report the unweighted CE and KL, their gradient norms, and their weighted contributions. “Small coefficient” does not guarantee a small regularization gradient. Keep temperature 1 for this first greedy screen, as in the existing pilot. Start from the original head, not the failed checkpoint.

For any state whose target label lies outside draft support, record an explicit mask and denominator; never map it to the nearest supported token or silently omit it. The separate vocabulary analysis should decide whether the observed missing mass makes this objective viable. If retaining a KL auxiliary on such rows, count and label those rows separately: it does not teach the unavailable target token. The offset mapping is documented at `experiments/qat-revisit-plan.md:57–70`.

The old draft can regularize tree score calibration and broad proposal support, but should not remain the main teacher. An alternative later objective is target-distribution distillation restricted to draft support, with the support mass explicitly represented in weighting. This can be useful for stochastic proposals or top-k branching, but adds choices about support normalization and teacher temperature. Do not combine hard target CE, multiple temperatures, ranking, feature loss, and several regularizers in the first run; an improvement would become difficult to interpret.

A greedy tree does not accept isolated top-1 matches in arbitrary rows. Correct ancestors must remain available in the tree. For a chain, accepted length can be written as the sum of prefix-survival indicators. A differentiable approximation using products of correct-token probabilities is possible, but saturates when any early probability is tiny and ignores discrete tree pruning. Therefore use CE initially, while reporting root hit rate, target-in-local-top-k, target-prefix survival by depth, selected-tree coverage, and score calibration. If CE improves without proposal coverage, a prespecified target-versus-competing-branch margin loss becomes more justified than more full-vocabulary KL.

The loss weighting should not accidentally optimize mostly doomed branches. First instrument reachability and normalize metrics by prompt/round/depth. Within the first controlled objective comparison, keep exactly the same state pool and weights for all arms. A subsequent state-focused continuation can balance root, valid accepted-prefix, and off-path auxiliary states, with an explicit modest off-path quota rather than pretending those rows are useless. Off-path learning can help when earlier weights change which prefix survives.

## State alignment and rollout: a training prerequisite

Capture `(prompt_id, round, parent_node_id, parent_path_token_ids, absolute_position, source_trajectory, target_seed, head_row, selected_tree_node, verifier_row, validity_mask)` and target supervision. Track padding, pruned nodes, EOS, and unmapped target IDs explicitly. A pruned head row may not have a corresponding verifier row; either replay its exact prefix for a target label or mark it unsupervised. A row index reused after top-k pruning is not a stable node identity.

Before optimization, compare a small frozen sample of root and deeper states against explicit prefix replay. Verify that state inputs, parent histories, attention/cache positions, and target labels agree. Fail training on incorrect alignment; do not treat one-token-shifted supervision as noisy data. Keep actual tree-verifier and canonical target differences visible because baseline parity is unresolved.

For head-only QAT, cached features are a legitimate first-order experiment: conditioned on fixed parent tokens and cache history, changing only the head does not change the body computation. Nevertheless its selected tokens alter subsequent tree inputs and future round boundaries. That means cached data from the original head is not on-policy after updates. A single scheduled refresh of training states under a selected student checkpoint is a cheap next diagnostic compared with implementing all-layer recurrence.

For body QAT, cached pre-head vectors are insufficient. The current trainable head detaches its input (`src/w1a1_eagle/qat_head.py:97–99`), and the general binary wrapper caches detached weights without a useful sign gradient (`src/w1a1_eagle/fake_binary.py:120–137`). Reusing either as an all-layer trainer would fail to train the intended recurrent computation. Wider QAT requires separate trainable wrappers, activation STE, frozen target feature extraction, and a faithful recurrent draft forward with masks/cache positions. The actual pinned AngelSlim training loss source was not inspected in this local analysis; no claim is made that a particular upstream recurrent loss is currently integrated.

If wider QAT is later justified, start with short unrolls at fixed teacher prefixes, then mix student-proposed histories while training only a bounded unroll. Preserve gradients through student recurrent hidden states over that unroll; detach at explicit chunk boundaries, not between every step. Token sampling/argmax is discrete and need not be differentiated in the first design: freeze selected histories per collection stage and learn on them. Increase rollout depth only after shallow survival and gradient stability hold. A soft quantizer or weight-only warmup can ease optimization, but finish with enough exact hard W1A1-forward steps and select only hard-forward checkpoints. Avoid claiming success during a soft/full-precision curriculum phase.

## STE and scaling: concrete risks and diagnostics

The current forward uses BF16 signs and mean-absolute scales; the weight scale is detached but recalculated on each forward (`src/w1a1_eagle/qat_head.py:83–101`). The backward surrogate is `1/max(alpha,eps)` inside `abs(BF16(w))/max(alpha,eps) <= 1`, zero outside (`:14–44`).

These mechanisms are confirmed by source, but their role in the failed run is a hypothesis:

- Large-magnitude entries outside the mask receive no direct gradient. With zero initial optimizer moments and zero weight decay, entries that remain outside the mask cannot begin moving through the sign surrogate. The row scale can move the boundary, and previously accumulated Adam moments can continue motion; therefore “all masked weights are permanently frozen” would be too strong. Log the active-mask fraction, transitions into/out of it, sign flips per row, and sign-flip reversals.
- The row scale is not fixed, despite being detached. Optimizer updates to latent magnitudes change next step's scale, but the loss gradient does not include that dependence. Log alpha drift separately from sign changes. A KL gain may partly reflect logit calibration rather than better target ranking; no existing report proves this occurred.
- The outer multiplication by alpha largely cancels the surrogate's `1/alpha` within the mask when alpha exceeds epsilon. Therefore this formula alone does not prove exploding gradients from small alpha. BF16 gradient arithmetic, global norm clipping, epsilon handling, and Adam's moments still require measurement.
- Sign reversals near zero may chatter. Check token-ranking stability across checkpoints, not only latent Euclidean movement. Do not average binary signs as the final model; an EMA of latent weights is an additional candidate requiring its own declared selection rule.
- Scalar token beta is positive and common to all head outputs. In an exact bias-free head, changing beta alone cannot change within-row argmax; it changes softmax confidence and can change cumulative tree scores/pruning. It is thus valuable to separate rank errors from probability calibration errors. Analogously, adjusting only positive final normalization gains cannot freely create a new activation sign code; it mainly changes the common activation scale unless channel signs change.

A later, single quantizer ablation could widen the STE clip from 1 to a prespecified value, retaining identical forward/export and objective. This tests whether gradient starvation matters without adding inference cost. Another possibility is explicit positive learned per-output alpha with sign latents, initialized from the original row scale. The native operation already consumes separate F32 row scales, so the arithmetic can represent it. But the current exporter recomputes alpha from the BF16 tensor (`third_party/llama.cpp/conversion/llama.py:17–38,422–432`); it cannot preserve arbitrary learned alpha automatically. Learned scales require an explicit exporter/metadata change and fresh parity audits, not an invisible trainer-only improvement.

Do not add activation thresholds, offsets, per-block scales, residual dense branches, or LoRA inference branches casually. Each changes the runtime contract and can consume the expected speed benefit. Existing dense body weights could be co-adapted to produce a better binary head input without extra inference nodes, but that is a wider recurrent-training experiment, not head-only reconstruction. Weight-only W1Ax outcomes should determine whether loss of activation magnitude is the main pressure before such changes.

A useful structural diagnostic is to group repeated activation sign fingerprints and inspect conflicting target labels. With identical sign vectors, row scales fixed across inputs, and positive beta, this bias-free head has identical per-row rankings regardless of activation magnitudes. A high rate of conflicting reachable target labels within such groups would be a limitation that head-weight optimization alone cannot remove. Exact collisions may be rare in 2,560 dimensions, so absence of collisions does not prove adequate capacity; nearby sign-code margins are also informative.

## Deployment fidelity must be checked before selection

The trainer's `_check_forward` compares against the same BF16 fake-linear implementation (`scripts/train_qat_head.py:307–316,543–556`). Its tests validate that contract (`tests/test_qat_head.py:16–49`), not native integer-dot equivalence. Native conversion instead calculates weight mean-absolute scales in F32 from BF16 source, and native activation packing calculates an F32 scale after a double-precision reduction. Native dots are integer XOR/popcount followed by two F32 multiplications (`third_party/llama.cpp/conversion/llama.py:28–37`; `third_party/llama.cpp/ggml/src/ggml-cuda/w1a1.cu:7–38,53–71`). The dense body also differs between BF16 Python and FP16 native tracks.

Perform fixed-input comparisons of exported signs, row scales, activation signs/scales, integer dots, scaled logits, local ranks, and selected tree proposals, including near-tie cases. Separately distinguish operator numerical differences from different inputs produced by BF16 versus FP16 bodies. A native-matching surrogate can use exact hard signs with integer-equivalent FP32 dot accumulation and native scale order; if adopted, retain a clear prior-contract control. Do not credit a training gain that disappears on the exported operator. Keep target FP16/verifier fixed in native testing even if the training target was BF16; disclose this domain shift.

## One bounded experiment ladder with ablations and stop gates

This is a suggested amendment to the existing one-run revisit plan, not permission to expand the active goal. The minimum version is Gates 0–1, arm B, Gate 3, then Gate 4. A reconstruction control and one refresh continuation make diagnosis stronger but are optional additional scope.

Budget for the full optional version: two fresh 500-step arms plus two 250-step continuations, at most 1,500 optimizer steps; each phase also needs a prespecified capture/evaluation wall-time cap since the old 39-second optimizer timing excludes expensive collection and online evaluation. Use one fixed seed for the bounded screen and do not present it as evidence of seed robustness. A later replication is justified only if the result is otherwise promising and explicitly scoped.

Under the existing plan, arm B should be the first new optimization run. The optional control can be run afterward to explain its outcome. Do not broaden to all-layer QAT merely because either head arm misses a 500-step deadline. The 24 final prompts must remain untouched until the candidate is frozen; the original 12 held-out prompts remain historical regression material only.

## End-to-end decision and counterarguments

For candidate C and anchor B, use measured `throughput_C / throughput_B = (emitted_C / emitted_B) * (round_time_B / round_time_C)` with consistently pooled emitted tokens/round and round times. The often-used `emitted = accepted + 1` needs EOS/token-limit verification; use observed emitted counts. Recovery of acceptance is necessary evidence, not sufficient speed evidence. Q4_0's lower logged draft-call cost means an equal-acceptance head-only candidate is not automatically competitive, but the missing verification decomposition and changing tree shapes prevent an unconditional threshold claim. Follow `docs/EVALUATION.md:57–85` for the measured bound and final comparison.

The strongest counterarguments are:

- **Head-only cannot fix quantized body recurrence or activation information loss.** Correct. It is the cheapest clean test of supervision and exporter fidelity, not the full solution. If the W1Ax study shows dominant activation damage or the head upper bound cannot beat Q4_0, the right outcome may be a negative result or a separately authorized body-coadaptation experiment.
- **Hard greedy CE can over-sharpen and hurt a tree requiring diverse branches.** Correct. Keep a small declared distribution regularizer and inspect branch/rank coverage; do not infer acceptance from CE. If the tree changes, compare matched policy rather than attributing all gains to QAT.
- **Cached target labels remain off-policy even after one refresh.** Correct. One refresh is a bounded diagnosis, not convergence of policy iteration. Fully online target-in-the-loop training costs more and is justified only after measurable benefit.
- **The prior run may simply have been too short.** Possible, but longer old-objective training is less informative than target supervision plus online selection. About one pass through cached rows is not a negative verdict on binary capacity. Conversely, thousands of near-duplicate rows are not thousands of independent prompts.
- **The STE or BF16/native mismatch could dominate everything.** Possible. Log those diagnostics and pass export gates now; change one surrogate or numerical contract only in a subsequent declared ablation. Simultaneously changing loss, scales, clipping, state pool, and export cannot identify a remedy.
- **Training to actual verifier labels risks learning numerical quirks.** Yes. Keep canonical target disagreements as a separate correctness diagnostic, target/verifier unchanged, and target-only ratios labeled appropriately. A faster verifier-relative pipeline is not automatically strictly target-equivalent.

The deliverable should be one reproducible candidate or one informative stopped recipe, with explicit causal uncertainty. No currently inspected evidence warrants a claim that all-layer W1A1 QAT will recover enough acceptance to beat Q4_0 on this EAGLE workload.
