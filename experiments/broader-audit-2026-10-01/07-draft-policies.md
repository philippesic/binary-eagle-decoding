# Adaptive drafting: cost-aware stopping before branching

Date: 2026-10-01. Audit baseline: project `f0bb92dda687d908b3cffe084510b3be757df54f`, native `b4e366d4f0a30cac07f14d51c54c5b1329b3f485`. This is source and primary-research analysis, not a new experiment. No model execution, accelerator work, large capture inspection, installations, source changes or reserved-final access occurred.

## Verdict and local evidence

Rank **online benefit-versus-cost stopping first**, calibrated prefix-survival estimation second, and shallow branching feasibility third. Another fixed depth/probability grid is not justified. The completed SM75 grid measured 11,520 requests; its best development-selected W1A1 cell reached 55.315 decode tokens/s against independently selected Q4_0's 98.068, or 0.564×. Selection and cross-cell timing drift prevent interpreting that difference as an isolated policy effect. Native confidence is top-10-normalized; the maximum is at least 0.1, making p_min=0.0 and 0.1 behaviorally identical. Raising accepted/proposed by discarding proposals does not establish more emitted tokens per second. Evidence: `experiments/w1ax-policy-grid-results.md`.

The RTX 5080 rescue traces identify why depth-sensitive control is plausible but insufficient by itself. Q4_0 accepted 1.0415 drafts per completed round, fitted-group D 0.4250, and attention/fusion rescue 0.6962. D's depth-two conditional survival was 22.1%, versus Q4_0's 50.1%; depth three was 13.4% versus 42.1%. Sequential feature/body drift makes later proposals especially expensive failures. Zero-draft-cost fixed-trajectory proxies remain below Q4_0's instrumented round rate for D (0.867×), row C (0.812×), and fitted head (0.964×); combined rescue has only 1.088× headroom. These are conditional CPU-wall, round-only calculations, not hardware ceilings or measured optimized throughput. Evidence: `experiments/binary-rescue-head-5080.md`, complete-round section.

## Native capabilities and frozen invariants

`common/speculative.cpp:864–1056` implements sequential EAGLE recurrence: sample top-ten candidates, choose maximum, optionally stop, then feed that chosen token and pre-norm state into the next body call. `common/sampling.cpp:714–741` independently samples the target at each reached prefix, emits it, and continues only on equality with the proposed token. Therefore the calibration target is native match/survival, not probability-ratio overlap. For deterministic proposal d under stochastic target sampling it is p(d); under frozen greedy evaluation it is a binary match. Importing sum(min(p,q)) labels would train the wrong controller.

The current EAGLE loop checks global params.n_max; the dispatcher truncates by per-sequence dp.n_max afterward (`speculative.cpp:3117–3120`). This cap limitation was already documented in the September throughput audit and is not a fresh optimization proposal. Completed unused-head pruning skips zero-logit catch-up head work; K/V-only catch-up, compact logits, shared packing and resident recurrence are separate existing mechanisms. The new contribution would be the adaptive decision, using their chosen common baseline fairly for Q4_0 and W1Ax.

Keep target weights/precision, sampler/grammar/RNG behavior, verifier rule, positional masks, rejected-suffix removal, deferred token/feature join, terminal counting and capture ancestry frozen. Controller inputs may include draft-side scores and past observed outcomes; current target draws must remain independent and cannot be consulted to choose an allegedly online proposal. Greedy sequence agreement and bounded stochastic distribution checks remain required after integration. Reduced verification shape can change numerical rows despite the unchanged algorithm.

## What primary research supports

[Mamou et al., Intel/Hugging Face dynamic lookahead](https://huggingface.co/blog/dynamic_speculation_lookahead) stop draft generation using softmax confidence. Their RTX 4090 greedy benchmark spans model pairs and summarization/code tasks; an MBPP example reduced target/draft calls from 38/192 at fixed five to 27/129 with an oracle. This supports context-dependent lookahead, not transferring a 0.4 threshold to top-ten-normalized binary EAGLE scores.

[Li et al., EAGLE-2](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.422.pdf) rank expansions by token confidence times ancestor confidence, then rerank the available tree. On Vicuna, Llama2-Chat and Llama3-Instruct across six tasks, they report 2.5–5× versus autoregressive decoding. The inspected paper does not identify a GPU model, limiting hardware transfer. Its well-calibrated ordinary EAGLE premise must be re-established after binary quantization.

[Miao et al., SpecInfer](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~zhihaoj2/papers/specinfer.pdf) combine multiple small models and use ancestor-specific tree attention. Their original four-T4, FP32 distributed OPT-13B/Llama-7B evaluation across five conversation datasets reports 1.9–2.7× lower latency. Their matching-length predictor substantially reduces small-model calls. Neither dedicated draft GPUs nor their tree verifier matches this project's concurrency-one native chain.

[Cheng et al., DSpark](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05147v1) calibrate cumulative survival and allocate verification budget using engine throughput profiles. Qwen3/Gemma offline tests use temperature-one ratio/residual sampling and count bonus tokens; scheduler-disabled quality results do not measure scheduler speed. DeepSeek-V4 production reports 60–85% faster per-user generation versus MTP-1, with unspecified accelerator SKU in the inspected paper. Its calibration and cost principle transfers; its TV acceptance labels and high-concurrency gains do not directly transfer.

[Zhang et al., Learning to Draft](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01639v1) jointly learn depth and verification-size policies with PPO and throughput rewards. A100 experiments span Llama3, Vicuna, DeepSeek-distill and Qwen3 on conversation, mathematics, instruction and QA tasks. Reported mean gains include 6.5% on Llama3 and 5.1% on Vicuna; acceptance-only rewards overgrow verification trees. Its up-to-30-GPU-hour policy training is disproportionate before this project's achievable headroom is established.

## Ranked candidates and two-day CPU deliverable

**1. Cost-aware recurrent stopping.** Estimate conditional native survival c_j from reached positions, giving prefix survival S_j=product(c_1…c_j). For candidate length k, expected emissions are E_k=1+sum(S_j), while C_k includes seed, recurrent steps, target verification, catch-up, repair and host/controller costs. Continue when the modeled marginal benefit satisfies S_(k+1) C_k > E_k delta-C; evaluate all bounded feasible lengths when hardware costs are non-monotone. Predict the next step's survival from currently available features: inspecting its logits has already paid that body call. Seed cost is sunk only for an intra-round choice, not for comparing draft versus target-only. History-only depth/context estimates are the initial model; use shrinkage toward pooled estimates and hysteresis rather than noisy per-request adaptation. Integration must avoid the next body decode, not merely resize completed proposals.

**2. Native confidence calibration.** Add a tiny depth-conditioned logistic/isotonic calibration over top-ten maximum, top-two logit margin, prior matches and context; predict survival labels only where verification reached that prefix. Report Brier/log loss and prefix reliability, leaving terminal-censored and unreached positions unlabelled. Compare against depth-only survival so a new confidence feature earns its cost. Existing round records contain stop_probability only at a stop, not every proposal's confidence. A learned confidence head or body-state classifier is a later step, not the two-day requirement.

**3. Shallow alternate proposals.** Prepare only a budgeted CPU tree representation and cost model, favoring one early sibling over deeper drifting suffixes. Current common chaining takes the first successful implementation rather than merging or reranking proposals; existing forced-token diagnostics generate one alternative through recurrence. Selecting one reranked chain can retain the verifier, but multiple body trajectories still cost time. True tree verification requires parent-specific target rows, masks, KV branches and sampler traversal, hence a separately approved verifier integration. Extra nodes increase target batch cost. Same-prefix top-k target-label coverage is an optimistic bound, not tree acceptance; sibling suffixes have different conditional prefixes.

Day one deliverable: a provenance-checked compact trace extract and an offline controller specification identifying supported states, censored labels and missing confidence fields. Day two: a pure CPU controller over supplied small records, emitting chosen lengths, predicted emissions/cost, calibration comparisons and explicit unsupported-state flags. This audit proposes that deliverable; it does not implement or test it. Keep development prompt splits by prompt, not timing repetition, and leave reserved-final prompts sealed.

## Counterfactual limits and decisive next gate

Existing round traces support same-prefix truncation reasoning and removal of logged suffix draft work. An oracle choosing k equal to observed accepted length can preserve recorded emissions and round boundaries, conditionally on identical target rows; choosing k below that length creates new seeds and recurrence that were never captured. Deeper extensions and alternative branches are likewise unsupported. Deterministic fixed-grid logs have no action propensities or coverage of every resulting prefix, so importance-weighted off-policy evaluation cannot repair this gap.

First compute an optimistic **fixed-ancestry** oracle: perfect stopping, zero controller/draft cost, and the lowest justified cost for required verification prefixes. Reject that bounded controller family if even this rate cannot beat matched Q4_0; explicitly withhold a global rejection when missing cost curves or unseen states prevent a valid bound. Do not subtract instrumented spans from uninstrumented request times. Later minimum GPU test: one frozen controller versus fixed policy on both candidate and Q4_0, plus target-only/FP16 anchors, 24 development prompts, five alternating repetitions, unchanged target/sampling, paired raw IDs, complete-round emissions and separate clean timings. Measure the target cost curve first; SM75 claims require RTX 2080 Ti.

Pursue adaptive cost control only after the optimistic gate admits useful headroom. Collect native prefix-survival evidence rather than repeat the fixed grid. Calibrate the verifier actually deployed. Keep branch coverage separate from measured tree acceptance. Reject unsupported replay claims before spending GPU time.
