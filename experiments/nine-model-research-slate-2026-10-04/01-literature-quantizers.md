## 1. EWGS-inspired weight-sign backward

**Highest-priority new probe.** EWGS multiplies the incoming discretizer gradient by `1 + δ·sign(g)·(continuous_value − quantized_value)`. Its matched ImageNet ResNet18 ablation improves W1A1 top-1 from 54.6 to 55.3 and W1A32 from 66.3 to 67.3. Fixed-factor CIFAR10 results support an inexpensive version: δ=10⁻³ reaches 85.3 versus STE's 84.7, while δ=0.1 collapses to 60.9. Full EWGS periodically estimates Hessian traces; that cost is unnecessary for the first screen. These are CNN results, generally retaining floating first/last layers. Accelerator hardware is not established by the inspected experiment description. [Primary paper, Tables 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.00903), [pinned author implementation](https://github.com/cvlab-yonsei/EWGS/blob/56c654cb893d53563eb352dd591d1450c34bdd15/ImageNet/custom_modules.py#L9).

**Local fit:** adapt only the backward of `_HardSignSTE` in `src/w1a1_eagle/recurrent_binary.py:41`. For current latents `z∈[-1,1]`, use normalized residual `(z−s)/2`, where `s` retains the existing zero-positive hard sign. Preserve the inclusive clipping mask, row-scale gradients, activation arithmetic and all precision exceptions. Packed export remains signs plus existing scales.

**Material integration detail:** the trainer shares one attached sign tensor across recurrent calls in `src/w1a1_eagle/recurrent_qat.py:369`, `shared_round_hard_signs`. EWGS is nonlinear in `g`, so scaling each invocation's gradient and scaling their sum are different recipes. Keep the existing sharing boundary and explicitly label the result **round-aggregated EWGS adaptation**. Do not introduce or remove sign reuse while testing it.

**Cost and failure cases:** small source change; elementwise backward work and potentially large temporary tensors, but no additional Adam moments or deployed metadata. Saving a new residual for all 218M EAGLE latents would consume roughly 0.81 GiB; computing it from the already-saved latent during backward can avoid that persistent allocation. Adam can cancel nearly constant gradient rescaling, so published SGD/CNN gains may transfer weakly. Excessive δ can suppress useful updates; it cannot revive a zero-scale row's absent task gradient. Preserve existing zero-scale behavior.

**Decisive development screen:** fixed-A8 EAGLE, frozen fusion initialization and balanced recurrent data, control δ=0 versus δ=10⁻³; same ±0.5 latents, optimizer, sharing, masks and teacher ancestry. Predeclare approximately 4,000 updates and a common accounted-time ceiling, reporting both exposure and wall cost. Require identical checkpoint-zero packed payload/native choices, explicit VJP checks at zero/endpoints/scale-zero and shared recurrence, then native development prefix survival and accepted drafts/round. Promote only a repeatable native improvement over the matched control; CE or extra flips alone do not qualify. Try δ=10⁻² only if the first factor produces a measurable, stable gradient effect.

**Disposition:** add as a concrete option under the plan's broad gradient controls (`experiments/nine-model-qat-research-plan-2026-10-04.md:168`), after higher-priority fusion/A1 admission. This is a refinement of that broad category, not a measured local improvement.

## 2. Late oscillation dampening

**Selective freezing only as a follow-on.** Nagel et al. add a detached-bin-target penalty that pulls latent weights away from decision boundaries. A matched 3-bit MobileNetV2 experiment improves post-BN top-1 from 69.50 to 70.37 with scheduled dampening; excessive constant regularization reduces accuracy. They also freeze repeatedly reversing weights to their dominant integer state. Their main evidence uses 3/4-bit CNNs, 8-bit first/last weights and unquantized normalization inputs. Importantly, they report approximately **33% training overhead for dampening**, versus negligible computational overhead for freezing; neither figure predicts this trainer. [Primary paper, Tables 4–8 and §5](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/nagel22a/nagel22a.pdf), [author dampening implementation](https://github.com/Qualcomm-AI-research/oscillations-qat/blob/9064d8540c1705242f08b864f06661247012ee4d/utils/qat_utils.py#L115), [author reversal tracker](https://github.com/Qualcomm-AI-research/oscillations-qat/blob/9064d8540c1705242f08b864f06661247012ee4d/utils/oscillation_tracking_utils.py#L26).

**Hypothesis:** some late EAGLE updates may change binary decisions repeatedly without improving useful prefix survival. This is supported only as a question: existing movement telemetry (`experiments/a8-qat-recovery/comparison.md:129`) observes histories every 100 updates. Its “flip-back” means return to initialization, not necessarily rapid alternating reversals.

**Local fit:** a binary adaptation can add a ramped penalty `mean((z−detach(s))²)` to selected sign tensors before `loss.backward` in `src/w1a1_eagle/recurrent_qat.py:791`, while retaining the existing [-1,1] projection. That penalizes optimization latents only; row scales and native export stay unchanged. Declare normalization per tensor/layer and λ explicitly—paper λ values are not portable to 218M latents and locally averaged CE.

**Cost and failure cases:** a few source edits and O(parameter-count) work, but actual memory/time must be measured. Dampening increases inertia and can prevent beneficial sign changes; it is especially inappropriate as an unconditional early-training default when only 5.3% of the corpus has been seen. This transformer uses normalization without the CNN running-BN-statistics issue, removing one major mechanism behind the published gains. Inspected hardware evidence does not establish a transferable device-level performance gain.

**Decisive development screen:** first observe update-resolution reversals for a bounded, fixed sample of sign coordinates during ordinary training. If persistent chatter coincides with unstable native decisions, fork one checkpoint with optimizer moments intact: matched continuation versus a modest late ramp in the penalty for 2,000–4,000 updates. Measure native root/early-prefix survival, acceptance, training cost and reversal persistence. Reject if reversals fall but native quality worsens. This tests a late-window stabilizer, not convergence of the future balanced long run.

Selective freezing deserves a separate follow-on only if chatter is localized and dampening costs too much. Full per-weight reversal/EMA/mask state is substantial; zeroing gradients alone does not freeze Adam latents because existing moments still move them. Frozen bits must remain consistent across forward, checkpoint and export; the current payload digest (`src/w1a1_eagle/qat_state.py:154`) derives bits directly from latent signs.

**Disposition:** conditional probe; defer freezing initially. No new normal-precision inference exceptions are required by the proposed local adaptation.

## Rejected tempting addition: ReCU

ReCU is already reviewed and deferred in the October 1 audit (`experiments/training-audit-2026-10-01/03-binary-optimization.md:46`). Its author code centers and standardizes weights, then clamps distribution tails; this can change calibrated initial signs and assumes a latent distribution unlike the current constant-magnitude initialization. Existing initialization (`src/w1a1_eagle/qat_optimization.py:153`) and projection (`src/w1a1_eagle/qat_optimization.py:283`) already prevent permanent out-of-STE-range death. Positive CNN results do not establish an additional local benefit. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.12369), [inspected implementation](https://github.com/yuanchunyu/ReCU/blob/0d748a6d45100ea2c054e999572f0585020e00c6/imagenet/modules/binarized_modules.py#L16).
